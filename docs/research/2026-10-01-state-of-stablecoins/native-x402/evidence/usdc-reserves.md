USDC is a digital dollar issued by Circle that is fully backed by US dollars and US dollar equivalents. USDC was developed to represent a US Dollar equivalent onchain, and is used to send, store, and receive money between people and businesses without the need for third-party financial institutions.

## FAQ

### What is USDC?

󰖢

USDC (USD Coin) is a [stablecoin](https://www.coinbase.com/learn/crypto-basics/what-is-a-stablecoin) designed to maintain a 1:1 peg to the U.S. dollar, aiming to provide stability in the [cryptocurrency](https://www.coinbase.com/learn/crypto-basics/what-is-cryptocurrency) market. Each USDC token is backed by reserves consisting of cash and short-term U.S. Treasury bonds. Introduced in 2018 by the Centre Consortium, a collaboration between Circle and regulated financial institutions, USDC seeks to bridge traditional finance with [blockchain](https://www.coinbase.com/learn/crypto-basics/what-is-a-blockchain) technology. It is widely used for transactions, [decentralized finance](https://www.coinbase.com/learn/crypto-basics/what-is-defi) (DeFi), and digital payments across multiple blockchain networks.

### How does USDC work?

󰖢

USDC operates as a stablecoin primarily issued as an [ERC-20](https://help.coinbase.com/en/coinbase/getting-started/crypto-education/what-is-erc20) token on the [Ethereum](https://www.coinbase.com/learn/crypto-basics/what-is-ethereum) blockchain, ensuring compatibility with Ethereum-based applications. Each USDC in circulation corresponds to a U.S. dollar held in reserve, with new [tokens](https://www.coinbase.com/learn/crypto-basics/what-is-a-token) [minted](https://help.coinbase.com/en/pro/getting-started/general-crypto-education/glossary/mint) as users exchange fiat currency for USDC through verified platforms. To maintain transparency, independent accounting firms regularly audit reserves, ensuring that the total USDC supply aligns with the corresponding reserve value. This system is designed to support price stability and reduce [volatility](https://www.coinbase.com/learn/crypto-basics/what-is-volatility).

### What are the potential use cases for USDC?

󰀎

USDC is used in various cryptocurrency applications, including DeFi protocols, [peer-to-peer](https://help.coinbase.com/en/coinbase/getting-started/crypto-education/glossary/peer-to-peer) transactions, and digital commerce. Businesses utilize USDC for payment processing, while individuals use it for remittances, savings, and [smart contract](https://www.coinbase.com/learn/crypto-basics/what-is-a-smart-contract) interactions. As a stable digital asset, USDC integrates into lending, borrowing, and trading platforms, providing a price-stable option for decentralized financial activities. Its blockchain compatibility enables seamless transactions across multiple networks.

### What is the history of USDC?

󰀎

USDC was launched in 2018 by the Centre Consortium to introduce a stable digital asset within the cryptocurrency market. The project was created to provide a transparent and fully backed stablecoin that could operate within the growing blockchain ecosystem. In 2020, USDC underwent smart contract upgrades to enhance its functionality for digital payments. Over time, it has expanded to multiple blockchain networks, increasing its adoption in DeFi and other financial applications while maintaining its 1:1 peg to the U.S. dollar.

### How can I buy USDC?

󰀎

USDC can be acquired through digital platforms that support stablecoin transactions. Users can send, receive, and store USDC in compatible cryptocurrency [wallets](https://www.coinbase.com/learn/crypto-basics/what-is-a-crypto-wallet). These platforms also provide tools to track USDC’s live price and compare it to market trends. Ensuring that the chosen platform supports USDC’s underlying blockchain network is essential for smooth transactions and asset management.

### How much does 1 USDC cost?

󰀎

USDC is designed to maintain a price of $1, functioning as a stablecoin within the cryptocurrency market. Its value is supported by cash reserves and short-term U.S. Treasury bonds that back the circulating supply. Minor fluctuations may occur due to trading volume or [liquidity](https://help.coinbase.com/en/coinbase/getting-started/crypto-education/glossary/liquidity-) conditions, but mechanisms are in place to sustain the 1:1 peg with the U.S. dollar.

### Is USDC always $1?

󰀎

USDC is structured to maintain a stable value of $1, backed by reserves of cash and short-term U.S. government securities. Market activity may occasionally cause small deviations in price, but transparency measures and reserve backing aim to ensure price stability. The design of USDC seeks to provide a consistent value across various market conditions.

### How much will USDC be worth in 2025?

󰀎

USDC is intended to retain its $1 peg through its reserve-backed structure. As long as its operational framework remains unchanged, USDC is expected to maintain stability in 2025. However, external factors such as regulatory developments or shifts in market demand could influence the broader cryptocurrency ecosystem, potentially affecting its adoption and circulation.

### What is the difference between USDC and USDT?

󰀎

USDC and [USDT](https://help.coinbase.com/en/coinbase/getting-started/crypto-education/USDT) are both stablecoins pegged to the U.S. dollar, but they differ in issuance and transparency. USDC is issued by the Centre Consortium, with regular audits providing insight into its reserve holdings. USDT, issued by Tether, also seeks to maintain stability but has faced scrutiny regarding the transparency of its reserves. Users may choose between these stablecoins based on preferences for audit transparency, reserve disclosures, and blockchain compatibility.
