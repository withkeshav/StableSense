# State of Stablecoins: reserves, payments and economic use

Published revision: 2026-10-02. StableSense release 3.8.0.

Stablecoins connect payment networks to reserve assets. Their supply, transfer volume and issuer Treasury holdings measure different things. None alone establishes commerce, adoption or additional demand for government debt.

This report publishes research reconciled on 2026-10-02. Independent challenges informed the underlying research; later corrections were checked against sources rather than treated as established by reviewer agreement. Research and publication review are not an audit or certification. The sources were collected on October 1 and 2; their observation dates are stated below. Publication does not make older observations current. Issuer statements, third-party estimates, model results and our inferences remain distinct. Unresolved findings are included as limitations, not confirmed results. This is informational research, not investment or legal advice.

## 1. Market size is a dated observation

Reap's compilation, attributing its market total to DefiLlama, reports $308.0 billion of stablecoin market capitalization as of August 13, 2026, 14.3 percent above its year-earlier level, about 99.5 percent dollar-denominated and 4.5 percent below its May peak. This is one secondary compilation, not an independently corroborated same-date total and not today's live market cap. We do not combine it with differently dated figures to manufacture a current range. [1]

Tether's Q1 issuer release reports approximately $183 billion of token-related liabilities and $141 billion of direct and indirect US Treasury bill exposure as of March 31, 2026, with an attestation prepared by BDO. Direct and indirect exposure is not a count of outright bills alone. An attestation is not an audit opinion. These historical issuer holdings are not net additional Treasury demand or a like-for-like ranking against sovereign holdings. [2]

## 2. Treasury demand depends on the money's origin

The Kansas City Fed explains that issuer purchases can be offset by sales by the investors financing stablecoin purchases. If those investors sell Treasuries at the rate issuers buy them, additional stablecoin supply need not increase total Treasury demand. Its illustrative household case can reduce demand when households forgo or sell direct Treasury investments. The funding mix is therefore a necessary input, not a detail to assume away. [3]

The Bulletin's revised HTML, identified by the publisher as corrected on September 22, 2026, gives a conditional issuer increase of $0.50 and bank reduction of $0.08 per dollar shifted. The publisher says the update corrected a calculation error in Treasury security holdings. Which specific calculation that correction addressed is not established here. The revised paragraph's components still subtract to $0.42, while the same paragraph states a net increase of $0.30. The discrepancy is $0.12. The linked PDF reader uses a $0.20 bank reduction, which subtracts to $0.30; its cache freshness is unknown. The HTML's parenthetical $0.42 concerns other bank assets, not an explicit correction of the net-demand result. Dollar/cents wording adds uncertainty. The intended coefficient remains UNRESOLVED. Neither coefficient is an observed marginal effect or our forecast. [3, 4]

For illustration only, the article's own scenario of a $650 billion shift from bank deposits into stablecoins gives $273 billion at the disputed $0.42 coefficient; applying $0.30 gives $195 billion. These calculations show the consequence of the source discrepancy. They do not establish either outcome. [3]

The Federal Reserve's December 17, 2025 FEDS Note distinguishes domestic deposit substitution, foreign demand and conversion from investment products. Stablecoins can reduce, recycle or restructure deposits. Paying for a mint from a bank account does not reveal whether the purchaser first sold a fund or Treasury security. [5]

Separate research provides evidence about yields without closing that funding gap. BIS Working Paper 1270's abstract, describing a January 2021 to March 2026 daily sample, estimates that a $3.5 billion inflow lowers three-month bill yields by 0.71 basis points on impact and up to 4 basis points within 10 days, with limited longer-tenor effects. These are an attributed econometric result, not an estimate of debt relief. [6]

BIS Working Paper 1219, published October 17, 2024, reports opposite aggregate responses of prime money-market fund assets and stablecoin capitalization to monetary tightening. New York Fed Staff Report 1073, revised April 2024, studies flights between stablecoins. Neither identifies the origin assets funding newly minted stablecoins. BIS Working Paper 1363, published June 23, 2026, models bank-lending and fiscal-space trade-offs; its calibrated outcomes are not observed fiscal dividends. Market-wide net additional Treasury demand, lending contraction attributable to stablecoins and fiscal-debt benefit remain UNRESOLVED. [7, 8, 9]

## 3. GENIUS: enacted provisions are not proof of commencement

The enacted July 18, 2025 text requires identifiable reserves of at least one to one under section 4(a)(1)(A). Eligible classes include currency, qualifying deposits, short-maturity Treasury securities, specified overnight repo and reverse repo, eligible government money-market fund shares, regulator-approved similarly liquid federal assets and tokenized forms of eligible assets. The Treasury clause specifies remaining or original maturity of 93 days or less. This is not a Treasury-bills-only mandate. The repo and reverse-repo clauses have different conditions; the full statute governs. [10]

The statute also provides for monthly public reserve-composition reporting, including average tenor and custody geography; restricts pledging, rehypothecation and reuse outside stated exceptions; and limits specified cash-equivalent, collateral and wholesale-settlement treatment for non-permitted payment stablecoins. These descriptions concern enacted statutory provisions, not a finding that every duty currently applies. [10]

Section 20 sets general commencement at the earlier of 18 months after enactment or 120 days after the relevant federal regulators issue final implementing regulations. Section 3(b)(1) separately delays the offer/sale prohibition until three years after enactment. This report has not established whether an early final-rule trigger occurred. Actual current commencement is UNRESOLVED. A proposal is not a final rule, and we do not describe the fallback date as locked. [10]

The OCC's December 12, 2025 Circle order grants preliminary conditional approval to the proposed First National Digital Currency Bank. It explicitly withholds final authorization to commence business until preopening requirements are met. The accompanying announcement names five conditional approvals, not an exhaustive current charter register. [11, 12]

CNBC's July 10, 2026 article separately reports approval for a trust bank named Circle National Trust, direct reserve management and no permission for commercial deposit-taking or lending. This report does not rely on the article's share-price figures. This is later secondary reporting, not a primary final authorization. The names and legal acts have not been reconciled here; Circle's current final authorization remains UNRESOLVED. Bridge's conditional approval is issuer-reported, with the original order and current final status also UNRESOLVED. [13, 14]

## 4. Open USD is live; supply is not adoption

Bridge's September 30, 2026 launch announcement identifies OUSD as issued by Bridge Building Inc. and live on Base, Ethereum, Solana and Tempo. Open Standard's launch page identifies Bridge as a Stripe company and names BlackRock, Lead Bank and BNY for reserves. These are issuer-side statements, not proof of realized partner payment use. Solana Compass separately reports the September 30 Solana launch and free one-to-one mint and burn; that description does not establish every user's total transaction cost. [14, 15, 16]

Bridge's reserve page at October 1, 2026, 11:30 UTC reports $468,445,399 supply, $257,214,691 cash and $211,230,708 Treasuries. The components sum to the reported supply. Computed shares are 54.91 percent cash and 45.09 percent Treasuries. The Treasury category includes money-market funds comprised of T-bill ladders with less than three-month duration. This is an issuer-reported snapshot, not an independent audit or a current live balance. [17]

Open Standard's September 24, 2026 governance description offers founding and participating partners an opportunity to earn equity based on supply and activity driven through their platforms. It describes a shareholder-representative board. Partner economics are not holder yield; company governance is not the same thing as control of the regulated issuer. This evidence does not establish direct holder ownership of reserve securities or a right to instruct reserve managers. [18]

Hypothesis: incentives could increase balances and qualifying payment use attributable to incentivized distributors. Testing requires partner-level balances, realized payouts, qualifying payment volume and a credible comparison controlling for promotions and market growth. Supply growth alone, a partner list or a flat integration count cannot settle that causal question. The adoption effect remains UNRESOLVED. [17, 18]

## 5. Reserve-income distribution is not a new category

Circle's unaudited Q2 2026 Form 10-Q ties distribution costs payable to Coinbase and Binance to USDC balances on their platforms. For the quarter ended June 30, reserve income was $667.733 million and distribution and transaction costs were $410.414 million. For the half year, the corresponding figures were $1,320.241 million and $815.816 million. Computed cost-to-reserve-income ratios are 61.46 and 61.79 percent. The cost line combines distribution and transaction costs; it is not entirely partner revenue. [19]

The filing separately discloses Coinbase distribution costs of $324.6 million for the quarter and $655.3 million for the half year. Coinbase's arrangement allocates amounts based on platform USDC after issuer retention and half the remaining broader-ecosystem amount after approved third-party allocations. It is a named arrangement, not a universal distributor rate. Accrued distribution costs were $105.799 million on June 30, compared with $119.038 million on December 31, 2025. Accrued costs are a balance-sheet stock, not another period expense. [19]

Inference: Open Standard participates in an existing category of balance-linked distribution economics rather than introducing partner compensation to stablecoins. Comparable Open Standard contracts, issuer retention amounts and realized net payouts are missing. Relative generosity, structural superiority and attributable adoption remain UNRESOLVED. [18, 19]

## 6. Transfers and goods-and-services payments are different

BCG and Allium's September 30, 2026 white paper reports more than $62 trillion of annual stablecoin transfers and $4.2 trillion of broadly defined real economic activity, or about 7 percent. Dividing its rounded inputs gives 6.77 percent. Its separate estimate for observable bilateral goods-and-services payments in 2025 is $350 billion to $550 billion. The authors call this a lower bound excluding important off-chain usage, including internal exchange settlements and stablecoin card payments. Against the rounded $62 trillion gross benchmark, the narrower range is 0.565 to 0.887 percent; this arithmetic does not convert the excluded residual into non-payment activity. We do not assert a market-wide 95 to 99 percent non-payment share. [20]

IMF Working Paper WP/26/52, dated May 2026, estimates a roughly 18 percent, $300 billion loss in incumbent payment-firm equity value associated with GENIUS passage, with sensitivity estimates of $220 billion to $470 billion. Its back-of-the-envelope scaling uses a relative price move and a prediction-market probability change. It is not Treasury demand or stablecoin supply. WP/26/44 is a different working paper, dated June 2026, reporting Treasury-yield and other asset-price responses to stablecoin demand shocks. Working papers are revisable research, not settled official conclusions. [21, 22]

## 7. x402 measurements must retain their scopes

The captured sources measure different populations and windows. They are not estimates to average into one market-wide commerce figure.

| Source and observation | Reported measure | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bitquery, August 2026; article September 6 | Five comparable EVM chains: about 18.3 million payment-shaped transactions and $2.59 billion, also rounded to $2.6 billion | Commerce-only volume or human buyers |
| Bitquery, same August population | $317 million after excluding one Arbitrum bridge contract from the five-chain total | A commerce-only residual |
| Visa and Artemis, as of April 21, 2026, since May 2025 launch | About $15 million adjusted volume and 109.6 million transactions for one implementation | The same population as Bitquery's August-only multi-chain series |
| x402scan capture, October 1, 2026 | 12,293,450 transactions; $974,215.14 after converting 974,215,144,043 raw USDC units at six decimals | A known observation window or common inclusion rules |

Bitquery gives $2.33 billion for Arbitrum and attributes 97.5 percent of that chain's value to one bridge contract. Removing that subset from the containing $2.59 billion five-chain total is valid; rounded inputs give $318.25 million, near but not equal to the publisher's $317 million. The unrounded inputs are unavailable. Applying Arbitrum's percentage to the entire five-chain total would be a denominator error. [23]

Bitquery also attributes 83 percent of the agent payments it found to two automated loops: 13.2 million payments on Base over 52 days and 5.7 million one-cent payments on Polygon. Chainalysis's June 3, 2026 article independently describes meme-coin farming and a pay-to-mint experiment charging 1 USDC with over 150,000 transactions in its first month. These source-specific samples establish automated and experimental activity, not one market-wide non-commerce share. [23, 24]

Visa and Artemis's exclusions and x402scan's unstated window make their totals unsuitable for subtraction from Bitquery's series. x402scan's captured unique-buyer and seller counts are 27,467 and 38,519, but address counts are not independently identified people or merchants. True commerce volume remains UNRESOLVED. Even a bridge-excluded and bot-excluded residual requires attribution to goods or services, with refunds, tests, duplicates and self-payments addressed. [25, 26]

## 8. Tokenized value is not market depth

CryptoRank's July 15, 2026 reporting, citing RWA.xyz, gives about $33.5 billion of tokenized RWA value excluding stablecoins in early July and a separate represented or pipeline measure near $345 billion. The definitions differ. The larger figure is not freely circulating market value. The same secondary reporting gives roughly 10 percent DeFi deployment; no independently reproduced denominator establishes that estimate here. [27]

BeInCrypto's July 2 article reports 910 of 1,289 tested assets above $100,000 with zero weekly transfers. The count share is 70.60 percent. It reports $32.9 billion without transfers and $26.2 billion active: the value-weighted share is 55.67 percent from those rounded inputs, consistent with its rounded 56 percent. Its report landing page separately dates data to May 31, 2026 and gives a $60 billion market base; $32.9 billion is 54.83 percent of that different base. Those denominators are not interchangeable. The precise transfer week, exclusions and full underlying dataset remain unavailable. [28, 29]

The publisher says about $27 billion of low-transfer value was represented assets, which may not be designed for public secondary-market movement. Zero weekly transfers therefore does not prove economically idle capital. An asset can earn yield or serve a financing function without public transfers. Economic idleness remains UNRESOLVED. [28]

No current primary BUIDL-specific AUM is established. Secondary figures describe different dates: approximately $2.4 billion for May 2026, $2.5 billion to $2.9 billion for early July, and about $2.7 billion for August. They are historical source-specific observations, not a contemporaneous disagreement range. Securitize's August 12 release reports $4.3 billion of platform-wide tokenized AUM at June 30 and separately mentions approximately $5 billion managed onchain. Neither is BUIDL fund AUM. [1, 27, 30, 31]

A tokenized fund share is an interest in a fund, not direct title to each underlying security. Payment tokens and tokenized fund interests can interact or substitute in some uses; different legal wrappers alone do not prove that they cannot. Product-specific terms govern yield, access, transfer restrictions and redemption. We do not assume a universal T+1 cycle for all tokenized funds.

## 9. What would change these conclusions

Net Treasury demand requires origin-asset flows, issuance and reserve allocations, displaced investor holdings and a stated counterfactual. The Kansas City coefficient requires a publisher reconciliation. GENIUS commencement and trust-bank operation require the applicable final rules and final authorizations. Distribution comparisons require comparable contracts and realized payments. Commerce requires transaction-level economic attribution. RWA depth requires the complete transfer methodology and utility evidence. BUIDL AUM requires a dated fund-specific primary disclosure.

This publication preserves uncertainty rather than filling these gaps with zeroes, forecasts or promotional conclusions. Citations and published arithmetic make the reasoning inspectable, but private capture retention is not full public reproducibility.
