StableSense Research - State of Stablecoins

The State of Stablecoins

Stablecoins are not just dollar tokens. They are becoming a layer of the financial system - a buyer of short-term US debt, a payments rail, and a dollarization force. This report tracks the wider landscape and the macro impact, sourced and dated. Where the evidence disagrees with itself or does not reach, that is shown too: a source that disagrees with itself is a finding, not a gap to be smoothed over.

03 - Who holds the world's dollars

A quiet buyer of government debt

Stablecoin issuers hold well over $150B in US Treasuries, mostly short-dated T-bills. Tether's reported ~$141B (Q1 2026 attestation, exposure as of 2026-03-31) exceeds the holdings of Germany ($91.3B) and Norway ($104.4B) per the US Treasury TIC Jan 2023 table; combined issuers' ~$182B exceeds Norway. The comparison is duration-mismatched: issuers hold short-dated bills, while TIC ranks total foreign holdings. And the figure above is gross exposure, not incremental demand: an issuer buying bills only adds to Treasury demand if nobody else sold them to fund the purchase. The section below separates the two.

Sovereign top 3 per US Treasury TIC Jun 2026; remaining sovereign rows per TIC Jan 2023 pending refresh; stablecoin issuers per their own reserve attestations (Tether Q1 2026, combined 4 issuers ~$182B). The comparison is duration-mismatched - issuers hold short-dated T-bills, TIC ranks total (long+short) holdings.

The GENIUS Act was enacted July 18, 2025 (the House passed it July 17, so "July 17-18" appears in coverage). Section 4 requires payment-stablecoin reserves to be at least 1:1, and the permitted assets are a list of eight classes, not Treasury bills alone: coins and currency; insured deposits; Treasury bills, notes or bonds of 93 days or less (the statute says 93, not 90); overnight repo backed by such securities; overnight reverse repo against Treasuries; shares of a registered Government money market fund invested solely in the preceding classes; other similarly liquid Federal Government-issued assets approved by the regulator; and tokenized forms of those classes. Section 4 also requires monthly published reserve composition including average tenor and custody location per category, and bars pledging or rehypothecating reserves outside its exceptions. The full list is reproduced in Section 8.

Commencement is not settled. Section 20 sets general commencement at the earlier of 18 months after enactment or 120 days after the primary federal payment stablecoin regulators issue any final implementing regulations. January 18, 2027 is therefore an outer limit, not a fixed date, and whether a final rule has already triggered the earlier date is UNRESOLVED in this report's research record. Section 3(b)(1) separately places the offer-and-sale prohibition three years after enactment. This report therefore does not describe any GENIUS duty as locked, operative or currently in force. Re-verify commencement before relying on these statutory duties.

See the full regulatory comparison in Section 8.

04 - Banks vs. stablecoins

Does this take money out of banks

When a household moves a deposit into a stablecoin, the bank loses cheap funding that funds mortgages and business loans. The research is genuinely contested, not settled. The diagram below shows the mechanism; the callouts below it give both sides, cited and neutral.

A $1 deposit moved to a stablecoin issuer does not disappear - it becomes a Treasury holding instead of bank lending capital. Distribution matters, not just aggregate liquidity.

Disintermediation view

Banks exposed to stablecoin flows lend less

NY Fed, Staff Report 1185 (Feb 2026)

First direct evidence of liquidity-driven disintermediation. Partner banks run "narrow" to absorb the volatility of stablecoin flows; their loan-to-asset ratios contract versus peers. The ECB (Schnabel, Jun 2026) draws the parallel to 1970s money-market funds draining retail deposits.

Minimal-impact view

Minimal lending impact modeled

White House CEA (Apr 2026)

Assumes a small baseline market. Criticized by Americans for Financial Reform ("a model built to mislead," May 2026) and the Consumer Bankers Association (Apr 2026) for treating recirculated funds as equivalent to retail deposits when they return as concentrated, wholesale, rate-sensitive liabilities that do not support relationship lending.

Both sides are politically situated. The honest answer right now: early evidence, no consensus number.

The yield debate

Should stablecoins pay interest? The GENIUS Act says no for payment stablecoins; the research on yield-bearing variants is contested.

Sources: CRS IF13173/IF13174; State Street (Apr 2026); BPI (2026, citing Cong, Chiu et al.); Federal Reserve FEDS Notes (Dec 2025).

06 - Dollarization

Dollarization from below

When a country's citizens choose the dollar over the national currency at scale, monetary policy loses its transmission mechanism. Stablecoins have turned dollarization from a government-level decision into a tap on a phone. BIS Working Paper 1370 (Jul 2026) found stablecoin flows show little response to capital controls, unlike bank deposits.

Stablecoin purchases as a share of GDP. Turkey is the only country with a measured figure (~4.3%, Chainalysis). Argentina, Nigeria, Lebanon, and Venezuela have strong qualitative and survey evidence but no single comparable GDP-share figure; ranked below Turkey on documented adoption.

09 - Who earns the float

The interest on your dollar, and who keeps it

A stablecoin issuer holds reserves that earn interest and pays the holder nothing. The gap between what the reserves earn and what the holder receives is the entire business. It is large, and the two biggest issuers report it in ways that are not comparable to each other.

Who gets paid, and whether that is new

Paying distributors for balances is not a new category of stablecoin economics. The interesting question is not whether it happens but whether anyone has compared the terms, and on that the evidence does not reach.

Tether publishes no line item called "reserve income". Its only net measure is a single line labelled "Financial result" inside the change-in-net-equity table of its attestation. Circle publishes an explicit "reserve income" figure. These are different measures, and treating them as the same number is the most common error in coverage of this market.

Tether's own documents disagree with each other on its headline. Its Q2 2026 news release leads with a positive "$1.5 billion net operating profit". Its own attestation, for the same period, reports a change in net equity of minus $3,171 million for the first half, and Q1 2026 was plus $1,040 million. That makes Q2 standalone about minus $4.21 billion. Both figures come from Tether; Tether publishes no reconciliation between them, and no Tether document defines the term "net operating profit" or states that unrealised mark-to-market is excluded. This is the single most load-bearing fact in this section, and it is invisible from either the press coverage or the release alone.

Circle, by contrast, is the simpler case and the more surprising one. Its reserve income is genuine and large, $2.64 billion for FY2025 (96.0% of its revenue), but it recorded a net loss of $69.5 million that year. Compensation expense of $844.9 million drove that, of which $424 million was stock-based compensation tied to its IPO. Almost the whole business is the float, and the pay bill took it below zero.

10 - The agent economy

Machines that pay, and the three clocks they run on

This is the most-reported and least-measured part of the market. Two things are routinely conflated: whether a payment rail is live, and whether the authorization layer above it is. A rail can be settling real money while the protocol that is supposed to decide whether a machine is allowed to spend is still a draft. The table separates them.

What a headline number actually counts

Every published figure for the same activity, with its publisher and what it measures. These are not one quantity measured more or less precisely. They are different quantities, counted over different windows with different inclusion rules and different chain sets, and the spread runs from thousands of dollars to billions. Do not average them and do not subtract one from another.

The median transaction is about one cent

This is the finding the totals hide. Agent payments today are overwhelmingly tiny machine-to-machine calls, not commerce. Any comparison against card network volume is comparing a plumbing layer against retail purchases.

What this pass could not establish, and is therefore reported as unknown rather than estimated: any first-party all-time x402 transaction count; any reconciliation between these headline numbers, whose scopes, chain sets, windows and adjustment rules differ and whose spread runs from thousands of dollars to billions; the true commerce-only share of x402 volume, which no source estimates on a common denominator; any production deployment of AP2; any 2026 Visa TAP or Mastercard Agent Pay transaction count; and a named news publisher serving paid content over x402 in production. That last absence contradicts the "publishers are monetising over x402" framing common in secondary coverage, and should not be reported as fact. What the measured samples do establish is that bridge traffic and automated loops dominate: one bridging contract holds about 88% of Bitquery's August value, two loops account for 83% of the payments it found, and Chainalysis attributes growth substantially to meme-coin farming.

11 - Who else is building it

The chains, the consortiums, and the funds beside them

Three things sit just outside the stablecoin market and get described as though they were inside it: Circle's own blockchain, a consortium token with more than a hundred named members, and the tokenized funds that hold the same Treasuries under a different legal wrapper. Each is reported here at the level it actually exists on, which for one of them is a press release and no supply.

The funds beside the stablecoins

Tokenized funds holding the same short-dated Treasuries as the issuers, under a securities wrapper rather than a payments one. A tokenized fund share is a fund interest: the underlying securities are owned by the fund, not by the holder. Sizes are the issuers' own published figures on the date shown, because the aggregators disagree with the issuers and with each other.

BUIDL: three dated reports, no current figure

An earlier version of this report presented a $2.70-3.52B "sources disagree" range for BUIDL. A range implies a currency and a single valuation basis that none of these figures has, so they are listed separately and no current AUM is asserted.

Reported value is not market depth

Headline tokenized value counts committed and represented assets. Weekly public movement counts something else. Both are stated here, with their separate denominators, and neither is merged into the other.

12 - Reality check

A rounding error, with momentum

After eleven sections of "this is a big deal," a deliberately humbling close. Today's tokenization is a rounding error against traditional finance. The growth rate is the story, not the current size.

Log scale, USD billions. US money-market funds ~$7.9T (ICI); US gold ETFs ~$530B (World Gold Council); fiat stablecoins ~$303B mid-2026 snapshot; RWA ex-stablecoins ~$20-40B (rwa.xyz); tokenized gold ~$5-8B. The ratios are structurally stable for now; the absolute sizes are fast-stale.

Until on-chain real assets approach even 1% of their traditional counterparts, claims that they are "changing" commodity or credit markets should be read as trajectory statements, not current-fact statements. The growth rates, not the sizes, are the story worth monitoring on the live dashboard.

Methodology

Every figure in this report was checked against multiple sources and cross-referenced against primary data. Where sources disagree on a figure, the disagreement is shown with each source and its date rather than a single silently-chosen number, and where no source establishes a figure the item is marked unresolved instead of being given a range or an estimate. A range is only shown when the disagreeing figures share a date and a measurement definition; otherwise presenting them as a range would imply a currency and a basis that neither has. Four claims were separately verified against primary sources on 2026-08-12 and are unchanged by the 2026-10-02 review: Treasury TIC data (Jan 2023), the GENIUS Act commencement mechanics (now stated with an unresolved trigger), CoinGecko Research on the USDC low ($0.8789), and CoinMarketCap/Reuters/ScienceDirect on UST (about $18B supply, about $60B combined loss).
